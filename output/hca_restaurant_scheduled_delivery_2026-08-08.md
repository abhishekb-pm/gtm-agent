# Scheduled Delivery: What Changes for Your Restaurant

**Applies to:** Restaurant partners enrolled in Scheduled Delivery
**Live from:** 14 July 2026 (Singapore) | 28 July 2026 (Kuala Lumpur) | 11 August 2026 (Bangkok, Jakarta, Metro Manila — pending Phase 2 confirmation)

---

## What changes for your restaurant

Customers can now place orders up to 24 hours in advance, specifying a 30-minute delivery window. Your kitchen does not see these orders the moment they are placed — the order arrives in your order management system 60 minutes before the customer's chosen delivery window, which is when you should start treating it like any other live order.

**What you need to do differently:** Watch for the "Scheduled" label in your order queue. These orders have a fixed target time, so late preparation directly affects the customer's delivery window. If you know in advance that you cannot fulfil a scheduled order — for example, due to an unexpected closure or a menu item you can no longer make — you must flag it at least 90 minutes before the delivery window. Missing that window has financial and account consequences (see [Flagging an order you cannot fulfil](#flagging)).

**What stays the same:** The order is accepted, prepared, and handed to the courier exactly as you would for any order. There is no new hardware and no new app. Scheduled orders appear in the same order management system your team already uses.

---

## How scheduled orders appear in your order management system

Scheduled orders are labelled **"Scheduled — prepare by [time]"** in your order queue. They will not appear until 60 minutes before the customer's delivery window — so an order placed at 9am for a 1:00–1:30pm delivery will appear in your queue at 12:00pm.

You will also receive a preparation reminder notification in your order management system at **75 minutes before the delivery window** — 15 minutes before the order itself appears in your queue. Use that notification to check staffing and ingredient availability before the order lands.

There is nothing else different about how the order looks. Items, quantity, and special instructions display exactly as they do on standard orders. The "Scheduled" label and the "prepare by" time are the only additions.

## Preparation timing

This table shows the full timeline for a customer who books a 1:00–1:30pm delivery window. The principle is the same regardless of what time window the customer chose — work backwards from the delivery window to find your preparation reminder and order appearance times.

| Time | What happens | What your team should do |
|------|-------------|--------------------------|
| e.g. 9:00am | Customer places the scheduled order | Nothing — order is not yet in your system |
| **12:00pm** **(T−60 min)** | **Order appears in your queue labelled "Scheduled — prepare by 12:45pm"** | **Accept the order and begin preparation** |
| 12:45pm (T−15 min) | Order should be ready for courier collection | Confirm the order is packaged and at the pickup station |
| 1:00–1:30pm | Customer's delivery window | Courier should have collected; delivery in progress |

> **How to read "prepare by [time]":** This is the time your food should be ready for courier pickup — not the time the customer receives it. Build in your normal preparation time from when the order appears at T−60 min.

**The 75-minute reminder:** At T−75 min (15 minutes before the order appears in your queue), your order management system sends a preparation reminder notification. This is your heads-up to check that you have the ingredients and capacity to fulfil the order. If you cannot fulfil it, this is your last comfortable window to flag it — see the next section.

## Flagging an order you cannot fulfil

### The process

1. Open the order in your order management system.
2. Select **"Unable to fulfil"** from the order action menu.
3. Confirm. The customer is automatically notified and receives a full refund.

You can flag "Unable to fulfil" at any point **up to 90 minutes before the delivery window** — this is before the order even appears in your queue. If you receive the T−75 min preparation reminder and know there is a problem, flag it immediately.

### What happens if you miss the 90-minute window

If you flag "Unable to fulfil" after the 90-minute cutoff, the system treats it as a restaurant-initiated cancellation. The following happens automatically:

- The customer receives a full refund
- The customer receives a courtesy voucher (funded by the platform, but attributed to your account)
- **Your partner account is flagged for review by the Partner Operations team**

Repeated late cancellations affect your account standing and may affect your eligibility to remain in the Scheduled Delivery programme.

**If your restaurant closes unexpectedly after a scheduled order has been placed** (for example, a sudden power outage), the system will detect the closure via your restaurant's online/offline status in the order management system. The customer is notified automatically and receives a full refund. To trigger this correctly, make sure your team marks the restaurant as offline in the order management system whenever you are forced to close early — do not leave the restaurant showing as open if you cannot fulfil orders.

### The timing at a glance

| Time before delivery window | Cancellation type | Customer outcome | Your account |
|-----------------------------|-------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| More than 90 minutes | Standard "Unable to fulfil" | Full refund | No penalty |
| Less than 90 minutes | Restaurant cancellation | Full refund + courtesy voucher | Account flagged for review |

## Eligibility — why you are or are not receiving scheduled orders

### Who is eligible at launch

Scheduled Delivery is available to opted-in restaurant partners who meet the following criteria:

- **Historical cancellation rate below 5%** — calculated on your last 90 days of orders on the platform
- **Active restaurant status** in your market at the time of launch

If your cancellation rate is at or above 5%, you will not receive scheduled orders until your rate improves. There is no manual override for this threshold.

### Why you may have been enrolled automatically

Restaurant chains (multi-outlet operators) are enrolled in Scheduled Delivery by default. Independent restaurants receive an opt-in invitation by email from Partner Operations. If you received this Help Centre article along with an opt-in confirmation, you have already been enrolled.

### How to opt in if you have not been enrolled

If you have not received an opt-in invitation and believe you meet the eligibility criteria, contact your Partner Operations representative or submit a request through the Partner Portal. The Partner Operations team runs opt-in campaigns per market — your invitation may be on a rolling schedule depending on your market's launch phase.

If you operate in multiple markets, each market's eligibility is assessed independently.

## FAQs

**1. Will scheduled orders affect my kitchen during the lunch and dinner rush?**

Scheduled orders appear in your queue 60 minutes before the delivery window — the same time you would receive any other order heading for that window. They do not arrive as a surprise during your rush. The T−75 min preparation reminder gives you an additional heads-up. Over time, scheduled orders may help smooth kitchen load, since some customers will schedule for off-peak windows to avoid surge pricing — but you will not see any change to how orders land during peak hours.

---

**2. Can a customer change their order after I've already started preparing it?**

Customers can modify or cancel their order up to 60 minutes before their delivery window. Since your order queue only shows the order at T−60 min, any modification before that point means you will see the updated order when it appears in your queue — not an earlier version followed by a change. Once the order appears in your queue, the customer can no longer modify it. If a customer contacts you directly asking for a change after the order is in your queue, treat it the same way you would handle any in-progress order modification request.

---

**3. What if I run out of an item in the scheduled order?**

Flag the order as "Unable to fulfil" as soon as you know, provided you are still within the 90-minute window. If you are past the 90-minute window, treat it the same way you would for any live order: contact the customer through the order management system messaging function, if available, and follow your standard out-of-stock procedure. If the customer cannot be accommodated, the cancellation will be treated as a restaurant-initiated cancellation and your account will be flagged for review. The best way to avoid this is to ensure your menu items in the system reflect actual availability, particularly for items with limited daily quantities.

---

**4. Do I get paid the same for a scheduled order as a regular order?**

Yes. Payment calculation for scheduled orders is identical to standard orders. The customer's payment is held at the time they place the order and released to you on your normal settlement schedule once the order is fulfilled.

---

**5. What if there is no courier available when the order is ready?**

The courier dispatch system attempts to match a courier for your scheduled order starting 90 minutes before the delivery window — well before the order appears in your queue. If no courier is found within 10 minutes of dispatch, the order is cancelled automatically, the customer receives a full refund, and you are notified. This is not treated as a restaurant cancellation and does not affect your account standing.

---

**6. A customer placed a scheduled order and also placed a regular order from my restaurant at the same time. Do I handle them together?**

No. The two orders are processed independently. Prepare and hand them over separately. Do not wait for both before handing either to a courier.

---

**7. What if my restaurant is closed on the day the scheduled order is due — for example, a public holiday I had not updated in the system?**

The system checks your restaurant's opening hours before allowing customers to book a delivery window. If your hours are correctly set in the system for that day, the customer will not be able to schedule a delivery during your closed period. If your hours in the system do not reflect an unplanned closure, make sure to mark your restaurant offline as early as possible — the system will detect the closure and automatically notify the customer and issue a refund, without penalising your account. Do not wait for the order to appear in your queue before marking yourself offline.

---

**8. I received this article but I do not see any scheduled orders yet. Is something wrong?**

Not necessarily. Scheduled orders only appear in your queue 60 minutes before the delivery window, so if no customers have booked a future delivery from your restaurant yet, your queue will look exactly as it always has. Additionally, during Phase 1 (Singapore, from 14 July 2026), only high-frequency GrabFood customers are eligible to place scheduled orders — the feature is not yet visible to all customers. Order volume on the feature will grow as the rollout expands. If you believe there is a technical issue with your enrolment, contact your Partner Operations representative.
